Which moments to lock in first, and why.

Most couples spend a year worrying about six songs and then decide five of them in the last two weeks. The order matters more than the calendar. Some choices set the mood of the whole day; some are almost interchangeable and can wait. Here is the sequence that saves couples the most stress.

1. The aisle first.

Walking down the aisle is the one song your guests hear before they know anything else about your ceremony. It sets the register — reverent, cinematic, joyful, playful — and the rest of the ceremony music follows from that decision. If you choose Canon in D you have quietly committed to a traditional ceremony; if you choose an instrumental Perfect you have committed to a modern one. Getting the aisle right anchors everything else in the ceremony.

2. Then the first dance.

The first dance is the peak moment of the reception, and it is the one song your partner cares about as much as you do. Deciding it early gives you months to grow comfortable with the arrangement, to shorten it if it runs long, or to change your mind twice. The First Dance Song Finder handles vibe, era, and lyric in five questions.

3. Parent dances after you know the room.

Parent dances are harder to decide in the abstract because they depend on what the room feels like. You have to know how emotional the ceremony was, whether the father-of-the-bride toast turned into a story about the dog, whether a parent isn't there. It is fine — often better — to leave these until you have your ceremony music locked and your reception timeline set. The Parent Dance finder has a tribute branch for the case that most other tools ignore.

4. Wedding-party and reception entrance last.

These songs are choreography, not statement. They should energize the room, match the vibe of the reception space, and clear in under two minutes. They can honestly be picked the week of, once you know who is entering with whom.

5. Cocktail hour is a set, not a song.

Cocktail hour is 45 to 60 minutes of ambient music, not a single choice. Decide the register — live piano, live violin, or a curated playlist — and the songs sort themselves. Most couples over-plan this and under-plan the parent dance.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Wedding Song Finder free?

Yes. Every quiz is free and takes about sixty seconds. Nothing to sign up for, no email required to see your picks.

Which wedding moments should I lock in first?

Start with the ceremony walking-in music and the first dance. Those two shape the mood of the day and are the hardest to swap late. Parent dances and reception entrance can be decided closer to the wedding once you know who is dancing and how the evening flows.

Can Julie play any of these songs live?

Julie's live repertoire covers 255 songs across 13 styles including classical, jazz standards, Motown, R&B, contemporary pop, country, and Taylor Swift. Result screens flag picks she plays live on violin or piano and offer a one-click path to inquire about booking for weddings across NYC, New Jersey, and Connecticut.

How does the Wedding Music Plan work?

As you complete each quiz your pick saves to a plan stored in your browser. A sticky bar tracks how many moments you have chosen and how many are left. You can share the plan with your partner or bring it into the Ceremony Builder, where matching moments pre-fill so nothing gets retyped.

Do I have to fill in every moment?

No. Every quiz stands on its own. Couples commonly do just the first dance or just the aisle. The plan is there if you want it and invisible if you don't.

What if the song I want isn't a recommendation?

All picks are recommendations, not requirements. Julie can also learn a custom song outside the 255-song repertoire with at least six weeks of lead time (custom-arrangement fee applies). Once you send an inquiry, include the song and she'll confirm whether the timeline works.

Where can Julie perform?

Weddings across New York City, Long Island, Westchester, the Hudson Valley, New Jersey, and Connecticut. Occasional dates outside the region on request. See booking details.
