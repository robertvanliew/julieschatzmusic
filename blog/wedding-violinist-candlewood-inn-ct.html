Wedding Ceremony Violin at Candlewood Inn, Brookfield CT

By Julie Schatz · July 24, 2026

On July 9, 2026, I played a 4:00 PM wedding ceremony at Candlewood Inn in Brookfield, Connecticut — solo violin, an hour of prelude and processional music on a summer Thursday. The Inn sits above Candlewood Lake in Fairfield County, and the ceremony space looks straight out over the water. Sharing a few short clips from the day, plus notes on what worked for a lakeside CT ceremony.

Why Candlewood Inn works for a summer wedding

Candlewood Inn has been hosting Connecticut weddings for years, and the appeal is straightforward: it's on Candlewood Lake, the largest lake in the state, and the outdoor ceremony spaces face west toward the water. A 4:00 PM start in July puts the sun over the couple's shoulders during vows, which is the kind of thing that reads on video and photos long after the day is over. For a musician, the venue is easy: reliable load-in, a coordinator who's done this a hundred times, and enough shade under the trees to keep instruments and gear stable for an hour of prelude.

Brookfield sits in Fairfield County, about a 90-minute drive from Manhattan and roughly the same from Hartford. That geographic middle-ground pulls couples from all across the tri-state — this particular booking had guests coming in from NYC, Boston, and DC — which is worth noting if you're planning a CT wedding and thinking about musician travel logistics.

Solo violin for an outdoor CT ceremony: what to plan for

Outdoor ceremonies in Connecticut in summer bring three variables you don't get in a Manhattan hotel ballroom: weather, ambient noise, and guest sightlines to the musician. None of them are hard to solve, but they change the equation from an indoor booking.

Weather. Wood and horsehair are sensitive to humidity swings. On a July afternoon on the lake, humidity was around 65%, temperature in the mid-80s. Solution: arrive an hour before start, tune under the same conditions the ceremony will run under, keep the instrument in the shade between prelude tuning and downbeat. For CT lakeside venues specifically I always ask the coordinator where the setup spot is well before the day so I can plan around sun angle.

Ambient noise. Candlewood Inn's ceremony space is close enough to Route 7 that occasional traffic carries. Acoustic violin projects well outdoors — no PA needed for a 60-guest ceremony — but for anything larger I bring a small battery-powered amp and a clip-on mic. That's the case at any of the outdoor CT venues I regularly play; a good rule of thumb: under 60 guests, acoustic-only. 60–150, add amplification. Above 150, plan for a full wireless setup and a soundcheck window with the venue AV.

Sightlines. At most outdoor CT ceremonies the musician sits off to one side rather than centered behind the officiant. This one had me stage-left about 15 feet from the arch — visible to the couple during vows, out of the frame for the photographer, and near a power outlet if I'd needed one. Coordinators usually know where the last violinist stood; ask them and it saves 20 minutes of on-day figuring it out.

Repertoire notes for a Fairfield County ceremony

The couple picked their processional in advance and requested one custom arrangement — a contemporary indie song reworked for solo violin. Pretty standard for the Fairfield County corridor, where the couples I book tend to lean toward Bon Iver, The Lumineers, Ed Sheeran, John Legend, Coldplay, and the softer end of contemporary pop for ceremony music. Classical still has a place (Pachelbel Canon in D remains the most-requested processional across every venue I play), but the trend I've noticed in CT over the last two seasons is couples building a ceremony set that mixes 30% classical with 70% arranged pop, indie, or R&B.

If you're planning a Candlewood Inn wedding (or any Fairfield County ceremony), the first dance song finder is a quick way to narrow down what fits the vibe, and the ceremony repertoire builder has the full list of what I have arranged and ready to play.

The best outdoor CT ceremonies I've played all share one thing: the coordinator built in a 15-minute weather buffer before the scheduled start, and the couple decided the ceremony was happening regardless of whatever the sky did.
